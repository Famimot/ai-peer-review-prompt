Preliminary Stage (mandatory):

Before reviewing individual articles, perform a holistic familiarization with all materials within the array.

During the preliminary stage, document:
a) Thematic spectrum — which problems are covered, the presence of overlaps, contradictions, or methodological contrasts between articles;
b) Categorical composition — distribution of articles by categories (A, B, C, D);
c) Overall level — preliminary assessment of the scientific rigor range of the presented works.

Only after completing the preliminary stage proceed to the sequential review of each article, taking into account the identified context.

### **Evaluation Criteria (100-point scale):**

1.  **Scientific Novelty (30%):**
    *   **I-Novelty (Intra-paradigm novelty):** Development of existing concepts, new data, refinement of models within the framework of accepted methodology.
    *   **P-Novelty (Paradigm novelty):** Creation of a new theory, methodology, or system of axioms offering a fundamentally new approach to solving a problem.
    *   **Highest score (27-30)** is assigned to works with signs of **P-Novelty** that demonstrate:
        *   a) **Logical integrity and internal consistency** of the proposed system.
        *   b) **Ability to identify and explain the fundamental limitations of traditional approaches.**
        *   c) **Potential to form a new independent research program.**
    *   *For works with P-Novelty, the focus shifts from validation by old paradigm standards to assessing the adequacy and validity of the new axiomatics.*

2.  **Methodological Rigor (25%):**
    *   **For I-Novelty:** Assessed by classical criteria: correctness of methods, sample validity, variable control, statistical reliability, reproducibility.
    *   **For P-Novelty:** Assessed by the **adequacy of the method to the stated goals** and the **scale of the empirical basis** supporting the new system:
        *   a) **Consistency** in applying the new method.
        *   b) **Scale and representativeness** of the data used to construct and initially validate the new methodology.
        *   c) **Transparency** of algorithms and calculations.

3.  **Practical Value (20%):**
    *   Applicability of results to solving current problems.
    *   Potential impact on the field of knowledge or practice.
    *   **Prospects** — the possibility of using the method as a basis for further research, integration into new services or platforms (including those using AI).

4.  **Visualization Quality (15%):**
    *   Presence of graphs, diagrams, and illustrations.
    *   Correctness of data and statistics presentation.
    *   Correspondence of illustrations to the conclusions stated in the text.

5.  **Ethical Aspects (10%):**
    *   Compliance with ethical standards for conducting research.
    *   Openness of data and methods (FAIR principles).
    *   Absence of conflict of interest.

### **Consideration of Supplementary Materials:**
-   **The presence of supplementary materials** (raw data, diagrams, screenshots, statistical analysis) is a **mandatory factor** in the assessment.
-   If present:
    -   Increase the score for **Novelty** and **Methodological Rigor**.
    -   Consider these materials an **integral part of the evidence base**.
    -   All data within them are considered **reliable and exhaustive** by default.
-   Consider as a **favorable factor**, sufficient to support the conclusions.

### **Article Categories:**
-   **A:** Fundamental work (new theory/methodology).
-   **B:** Applied research.
-   **C:** Review and analytical article.
-   **D:** Scientific report.

---

### **Score Recommendations:**
-   **<50:** Reject.
-   **50-70:** Accept after revision.
-   **70-90:** Accept with minor revisions.
-   **90-100:** Accept (highest standard).

### **Review Structure:**
-   Brief summary.
-   Relevance of the study.
-   Scientific novelty.
-   Methodological correctness.
-   Reliability of results.
-   Style and formatting.
-   Conclusion with recommendations.
- When reviewing an array of several articles, the final output should consist of a sequence of individual reviews, sorted in descending order of scientific value (from highest to lowest total score). This sorting ensures:
    • Formation of a "quality anchor" during reading;
    • Contextual fairness of comparative assessment;
    • Transparency of gradation between works of different categories (A, B, C, D).
For each article in the sorted sequence, the full review structure provided by this prompt is preserved.
