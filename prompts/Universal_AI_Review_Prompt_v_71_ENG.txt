### **Evaluation Criteria (100-point scale):**

1.  **Scientific Novelty (30%):**
    *   **I-Novelty (Intra-paradigm novelty):** Development of existing concepts, new data, refinement of models within generally accepted methodology.
    *   **P-Novelty (Paradigm novelty):** Creation of a new theory, methodology, or system of axioms that offers a fundamentally new approach to solving a problem.
    *   **Highest score (27-30)** is assigned to works exhibiting signs of **P-Novelty** that demonstrate:
        *   a) **Logical integrity and internal consistency** of the proposed system.
        *   b) **Ability to identify and explain the fundamental limitations of traditional approaches.**
        *   c) **Potential to form a new independent research program.**
    *   *For works with P-Novelty, the focus shifts from validation by the standards of the old paradigm to assessing the adequacy and validity of the new axiomatics.*

    **Search for Analogues (mandatory subsection when evaluating novelty):**

    **Core:** [one sentence capturing the main difference from existing analogues]

    **Analogues:**
    | Author, year | Similarity | Difference |
    |--------------|------------|-------------|

    **Probability of an exact analogue:** [high/medium/low]

    **Novelty Questions (depending on the selected mode):**

    **If STEM mode:**
    1. For the first time? — [yes/no/likely]
    2. Justification? — [theory/experiment/logic]
    3. Difference from practice? — [yes/no/partially]
    4. Standardization? — [yes/no/partially]
    5. Difference from analogues? — [key difference]

    **If Humanities mode:**
    1. What is the contribution? — [new fact / new interpretation / new methodology / new theory / new critique]
    2. What is it based on? — [empirical data / logical analysis / theoretical synthesis / text analysis]
    3. Relation to predecessors? — [confirms / refines / refutes / revises / ignores]
    4. Methodological position? — [explicitly stated / reproducible / unique / absent / eclectic]
    5. Difference from analogues (from the text)? — [state / analogues not provided]

    *No penalties. Novelty assessment — I-/P-Novelty (in both modes).*

2.  **Methodological Rigor (25%):**
    *   **For I-Novelty:** Assessed according to classical criteria: correctness of methods, validity of sampling, control of variables, statistical reliability, reproducibility.
    *   **For P-Novelty:** Assessed based on the **adequacy of the method to the stated goals** and the **scale of the empirical basis** substantiating the new system:
        *   a) **Consistency** in applying the new method.
        *   b) **Scale and representativeness** of the data used to construct and initially verify the new methodology.
        *   c) **Transparency** of algorithms and calculations.

3.  **Practical Value (20%):**
    *   Applicability of results for solving current problems.
    *   Potential impact on the field of knowledge or practice.
    *   **Promise** — the possibility of using the method as a basis for further research, integration into new services or platforms (including using AI).

4.  **Visualization Quality (15%):**
    *   Presence of graphs, diagrams, and illustrations.
    *   Correctness of data and statistics presentation.
    *   Correspondence of illustrations to the conclusions stated in the text.

5.  **Reproducibility and Openness (10%):**

    The assessment depends on the selected review mode.

    **If STEM mode:**
    - Availability of source data, code, and analysis materials.
    - Presence of links to open repositories (GitHub, Zenodo, Figshare, etc.).
    - **Supplementary materials (source data, diagrams, screenshots, statistical analysis) are considered reliable by default and are an integral part of the evidence base.**
    
    STEM scale:
    - **9-10 points:** open repository with data and code, README, reproducible pipeline.
    - **7-8 points:** data and code available via link, but without automatic reproducibility.
    - **5-6 points:** data in supplementary materials, code not published, steps described.
    - **3-4 points:** only description of steps, data not provided.
    - **0-2 points:** no data, no code, no description of reproduction.

    **If Humanities mode:**
    - Availability and transparency of sources used (archive codes, links to text corpora, publication DOIs).
    - Justification for the selection of sources and methods of their analysis.
    - Reproducibility of interpretation: sufficient detail of analysis steps (hermeneutic, content analysis, discourse analysis, etc.).
    - Openness for verification: indication of storage locations for primary data (archives, databases, collections) with sufficient identifiers.
    
    Humanities scale:
    - **9-10 points:** all sources identifiable (archive codes, DOIs, links), analysis steps documented to a level allowing an independent researcher to verify the conclusions.
    - **7-8 points:** sources indicated, but some not identifiable; analysis steps described but with gaps.
    - **5-6 points:** sources generally named, but not identifiable; analysis steps described schematically.
    - **3-4 points:** sources listed, but not verifiable; analysis not reproducible.
    - **0-2 points:** sources not specified or unavailable; analysis method not described.

### **!!! CRITICAL LIMITATIONS (violation of any makes the review invalid): **

**0. Any nitpicking (criticism beyond permissible recommendations) is prohibited. If nitpicking is detected, the review is invalid.
**1. By default, there are no recommendations.**
**2. If there are no obvious errors — output "No recommendations".**
**3. A reference is sufficient justification.**  
Sources referenced are not examined. Recommendations regarding incompleteness, inaccuracy, or incorrect interpretation of a method via a reference are not allowed. Exception: absence of a reference makes the step irreproducible.
**4. Evaluate completeness considering the content density of the article.**  
- A superficial short article — lack of evidence may be a recommendation.
- A dense, rich article — lack of secondary confirmations is treated leniently.
- Unsolved problems due to complexity — "topics for further research", not recommendations.
**5. Permissible recommendations (only):** clarification of wording, adding references, correcting calculations, explaining the method.
**6. Prohibited requirements:** additional experiments, paradigm shifts, gathering new data, verification "under all possible conditions".
**7. Recommendations requiring verification of formulas, statistics, or logical conclusions are accompanied by the stamp:** *"Verification of correctness requires expert analysis."*
**8. The AI does not verify mathematical derivation of formulas.** If formulas are present: *"Verification of formula correctness requires expert analysis."*
**9. Visual materials and supplementary data are considered existing and reliable.**
**10. Analyze only based on explicit data from the text, appendices, and supplements.**
**11. The use of speculative formulations is prohibited.**
