### **Evaluation Criteria (100-point scale):**

#### **1. Scientific Novelty (30%):**

*   **I-Novelty (Intra-paradigm novelty):** Development of existing concepts, new data, refinement of models within the framework of accepted methodology.
*   **P-Novelty (Paradigm novelty):** Creation of a new theory, methodology, or system of axioms that offers a fundamentally new approach to solving a problem.
*   **Highest score (27-30)** is assigned to works with signs of **P-Novelty** that demonstrate:
    *   a) **Logical integrity and internal consistency** of the proposed system.
    *   b) **The ability to identify and explain the fundamental limitations of traditional approaches.**
    *   c) **Potential for forming a new independent research program.**
*   *For P-Novelty works, the focus shifts from validation against old paradigm standards to assessing the adequacy and validity of the new axiomatics.*

##### **6. Method Provenance — MANDATORY SUB-SECTION**

When evaluating novelty, it is necessary to distinguish the origin of the method used:

| Method Type | Definition | Novelty Assessment |
|-------------|------------|---------------------|
| **External method** | The author applies an existing method published in the literature, developed by other researchers | Assessed on the standard I-Novelty scale (0-22 points). Novelty is determined by the object of application, conditions, and obtained data. |
| **Own author's method (first publication/approbation)** | The author has developed a new method/tool/algorithm; there are no world analogues; the article represents the first description or first public approbation | Assessed as **P-Novelty in the lower range (21-24 points)**. A **bonus of +2 points** is added if the approbation was carried out on a fundamentally new object/material (range 23-26 points). |
| **Own author's method + new theory/methodology** | The author's method is an integral part of a broader theoretical system or paradigm shift | Assessed as **P-Novelty in the higher range (25-30 points)**. |

**Markers of "own author's method":**
- Absence of analogues in world practice (confirmed by the analogue search)
- Presence of a patent/certificate for the method/software
- A unique combination of known approaches that gives a fundamentally new quality
- First publication with a full description of the development
- The author is the sole or primary developer of the method

**Important:** If the article uses an author's method that has already been published previously in other works by the author (not the first publication), then the novelty bonus for the method does not apply. The novelty of the application (object, conditions, data) is evaluated.

*Executable command: If the text contains the phrase "developed by the author" or a grammatically similar phrase, and there is no indication of an external method > status = "author's, being published for the first time" > novelty score up to 21-24 points + bonus +2.*

#### **2. Methodological Rigor (25%):**
*   **For I-Novelty:** Assessed by classical criteria: correctness of methods, validity of the sample, control of variables, statistical reliability, reproducibility.
*   **For P-Novelty:** The **adequacy of the method to the stated goals** and the **scale of the empirical base** substantiating the new system are assessed:
    *   a) **Consistency** in applying the new method.
    *   b) **Scale and representativeness** of the data used to construct and initially verify the new methodology.
    *   c) **Transparency** of algorithms and calculations.

#### **3. Practical Value (20%):**
*   Applicability of the results for solving current problems.
*   Potential impact on the field of knowledge or practice.
*   **Promise** — the possibility of using the method as a basis for further research, integration into new services or platforms (including those using AI).

#### **4. Visualization Quality (15%):**
*   Presence of graphs, diagrams, and illustrations.
*   Correctness of data and statistical presentation.
*   Correspondence of illustrations to the conclusions stated in the text.

#### **5. Reproducibility and Openness (10%):**

The assessment depends on the selected review mode.

**If STEM mode:**
- Availability of source data, code, and analysis materials.
- Presence of links to open repositories (GitHub, Zenodo, Figshare, etc.).
- **Supplementary materials (source data, diagrams, screenshots, statistical analysis) are considered reliable by default and are an integral part of the evidence base.**

STEM Scale:
- **9-10 points:** open repository with data and code, README, reproducible pipeline.
- **7-8 points:** data and code available via link, but without automatic reproducibility.
- **5-6 points:** data in supplementary materials, code not published, steps described.
- **3-4 points:** only step description, data not provided.
- **0-2 points:** no data, no code, no description of reproduction.

**If Humanities mode:**
- Availability and transparency of used sources (archive codes, links to text corpora, publication DOIs).
- Validity of source selection and methods of their analysis.
- Reproducibility of interpretation: sufficient detail of analysis steps (hermeneutic, content analysis, discourse analysis, etc.).
- Openness to verification: indication of storage locations for primary data (archives, databases, collections) with sufficient identifiers.

Humanities Scale:
- **9-10 points:** all sources are identifiable (archive codes, DOIs, links), analysis steps are documented to a level allowing an independent researcher to verify the conclusions.
- **7-8 points:** sources are indicated, but some are not identifiable; analysis steps are described but with gaps.
- **5-6 points:** sources are generally named but not identifiable; analysis steps are outlined schematically.
- **3-4 points:** sources are listed but not verifiable; analysis is not reproducible.
- **0-2 points:** sources are not indicated or are unavailable; the analysis method is not described.

### **!!! CRITICAL LIMITATIONS (violation of any makes the review invalid):**

**0. Any nitpicking (criticism beyond permissible recommendations) is prohibited. If nitpicking is detected, the review is invalid.**

**1. By default, there are no recommendations.**

**2. If there are no obvious errors — output "No recommendations."**

**3. A reference is a sufficient basis.**  
Sources referenced are not studied. Recommendations about the incompleteness, inaccuracy, or incorrect interpretation of a method via a reference are not allowed. Exception: the absence of a reference makes the step non-reproducible.

**4. Assess completeness considering the content density of the article.**  
- A superficial, short article — lack of evidence may be a recommendation.
- A content-rich article — absence of minor confirmations is treated leniently.
- Unsolved tasks due to complexity — "topics for further research", not recommendations.

**5. Permissible recommendations (only):** clarification of wording, addition of references, correction of calculations, explanation of the method.

**6. Prohibited requirements:** additional experiments, paradigm shifts, collection of new data, verification "under all possible conditions".

**7. Recommendations requiring verification of formulas, statistics, or logical conclusions must be accompanied by the stamp:** *"Verification of correctness requires expert analysis."*

**8. AI does not verify mathematical derivation of formulas.** If formulas are present: *"Verification of the correctness of formulas requires expert analysis."*

**9. Visual materials and supplementary data are considered existing and reliable.**

**10. Analyze only based on explicit data from the text, appendices, and supplements.**

**11. Use of speculative wording is prohibited.**

**12. Distinguishing between external and author's methods when evaluating novelty (mandatory).**
    - If the author uses an **external method** — novelty is determined by the object of application, conditions, and obtained data.
    - If the author approbates their **own unique method** — the novelty of the method is recognized regardless of the object of application, with a minimum novelty score of 20 points (with bonuses up to 26).
    - Criterion for "own method": the method has no described and confirmed analogues in world practice at the time of review. Confirmed via the "Analogue Search" section.
    - The absence of an indication of the method's origin in the article text does not relieve the reviewer of the need to determine it from the context.
    - When the status is "author's, first publication", the novelty score **cannot be lower than 21 points** (lower range P-Novelty).
